240	Scam?
241	Contains a link
242	Scammers send links to fake websites where they want your card details or passwords.
243	Shortened link
244	A shortened link hides where it really takes you.
245	Wants card details, a password or a code
246	Your bank, the post office or an authority will never ask you for these details in a message.
247	Pushes you to hurry
248	Hurry and fear are scammers' main weapons. Real institutions give you time.
249	Wants you to pay something
250	Scammers often want a "fee", an extra charge or a payment. It's a pretext to get your money or card details.
251	Pretends to be a parcel delivery
252	Fake parcel messages are one of the most common scams.
253	Pretends to be a bank, authority or company
254	Scammers like to pose as trustworthy institutions. Check it yourself on the official number.
255	Asks you to transfer money
256	No real banker or police officer will ask you to transfer money to a "safe account".
257	Promises a prize or gift
258	If you didn't enter a competition, you can't have won anything.
259	Pretends to be a family member
260	The common trick "Hi Mum, I have a new number". Call your relative on their old number.
261	Asks for money
262	Whenever a message asks you for money, check the sender another way – ideally by calling a number you know.
263	Wants you to install something
264	Remote access apps give the scammer control over your device.
265	Promises quick profit
266	Guaranteed high returns don't exist. This is a typical investment scam.
